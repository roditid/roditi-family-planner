# Pickup Planner — Design Context

## Users
A family of 6: Paula & Dani (parents/admins), Adam, Liam, Yali (kids), and four caregivers (3 grandparents nicknamed Vovo / Tataia / Nonna, plus Liezel the nanny). Living in Tel Aviv. The grandparents are the **primary surface area** — they tap a personal link, see the week, and claim a pickup. Friction kills usage.

## Use Cases
- Weekly: helper opens their personal link → sees what's on for the week → claims one or two pickups they can do
- Daily: assigned helper gets a morning email with child, time, and exact pickup/dropoff location, including ganenet phone numbers
- Admin: parents see who's on what, fill gaps, set per-activity defaults

## Brand Personality
**Warm, family, editorial.** Not a productivity tool — closer to a family photo album that happens to have a calendar in it. Real kid faces are the hero, not abstract color tags. Calm, never busy. Should feel like something a thoughtful sibling made, not enterprise software.

## Aesthetic Direction
- **Visual tone:** Cream-and-sage, with coral/clay accents. Warm, slightly editorial, hand-finished. Real photographs of the kids as the dominant graphic element.
- **Typography:** Serif display for warmth; restrained sans body for legibility. Big tabular times (it's a calendar). Generous tracking on small caps labels.
- **Density:** Mobile-first generous; desktop denser with more inline information so glance-scanning the week works.
- **Anti-references:** Generic SaaS dashboards, neon dark mode, gradients-as-decoration, glassmorphism, tiny cards in stat-grids, clinical date-pickers.
- **Theme:** Light only. The app is used in daylight; warm cream background reads better than dark for older eyes.

## Design Principles
1. **Photos lead.** A child's face is more identifying than any color tag — show it big.
2. **Time is the headline.** Grandparents scan by time, then by who, then by what. Hierarchy reflects that order.
3. **No friction for helpers.** Tap link → in. Never ask anyone to type, sign in, or read instructions.
4. **Information, not decoration.** Every accent stripe / icon / pill should carry meaning. No gloss for gloss's sake.
5. **One density per surface.** Mobile = generous and roomy. Desktop = denser, more inline detail visible without taps.
